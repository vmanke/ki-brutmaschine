# Spec: a Soul that learns, imagines and meets changing goals (arms I, G and IG)

Status: needs-triage. A design for the owner. Nothing is built.

It follows the owner's choice of 2026-11-04, after the screens of the grid (ticket 03) and of
arm W (ticket 02). The owner chose two of the proposed next steps: weights that learn from the
success of children, and goals that change. The reason, in the owner's words: "a und b, weil
Eltern und Kinder an ihrer Umwelt lernen und das auf Neues projizieren" (because parents and
children learn from their environment and project it onto what is new). The owner then added
imagination: "Vorstellungskraft misst die Wirkung des Ausprobierens adaptierter Gedanken"
(imagination measures the effect of trying out adapted thoughts).

## 1 What the screens showed

- In arm W, the birth Soul gave each family a fixed preference. The preferences did not turn
  towards Linear, Phase and Hadamard (`screen-w.md`, criterion 2).
- Arm WF had a fixed, hand-made preference for these three families. It protected them, and it
  passed before W on 8 of 8 seeds. So preferences help when they point the right way.
- The pass waits for one Worker that names Hadamard, Phase and Linear. No phrase names two of
  them (`src/cascade/circuit_genes.rs`, lines 67 to 71), so such a Worker needs three sections at
  once. The seeds reach only swap and cs, so 9 targets are open, and the pass needs 5 of them.
  Without that Worker, only cz, ch and ccz can be reached (`src/cascade/circuit.rs`, the routes
  of the library targets).

## 2 Sources

Checked in this session (bibliographic record and abstract):

- Learning during life lets evolution find a "needle in a haystack", even when nothing learned is
  inherited. It is "like searching for a needle in a haystack when someone tells you when you are
  getting close". Hinton, G. E. and Nowlan, S. J. (1987), How learning can guide evolution,
  Complex Systems 1, 495 to 502.
- Goals that change over time can speed up evolution. The largest speedup comes from modularly
  varying goals, where each new goal shares some sub-problems with the previous one. The harder
  the goal, the larger the speedup. The goals changed every E = 20 generations. Kashtan, N.,
  Noor, E. and Alon, U. (2007), Varying environments can speed up evolution, PNAS 104(34),
  13711 to 13716, DOI 10.1073/pnas.0611630104.
- An agent can be trained inside the environment generated by its own learned world model, and
  the result carries back to the real environment. To keep the agent from exploiting errors of
  the model, the authors make the imagined environment noisier. Ha, D. and Schmidhuber, J.
  (2018), Recurrent World Models Facilitate Policy Evolution, NeurIPS 2018.
- In evolutionary computation, such models are surrogates of the fitness. They should be used
  together with the real fitness, or the search can be misled by a false optimum. Jin, Y.
  (2011), Surrogate-assisted evolutionary computation: recent advances and future challenges,
  Swarm and Evolutionary Computation 1(2), 61 to 70, DOI 10.1016/j.swevo.2011.05.001.
- The brain imagines the future from remembered experience. Schacter, D. L., Addis, D. R. and
  Buckner, R. L. (2007), Remembering the past to imagine the future: the prospective brain,
  Nature Reviews Neuroscience 8(9), 657 to 661 (PubMed 17700624).

Classic sources, not checked again in this session:

- Learning from the error of a prediction is the delta rule: Widrow, B. and Hoff, M. E. (1960),
  Adaptive switching circuits, IRE WESCON Convention Record 4, 96 to 104; Rescorla, R. A. and
  Wagner, A. R. (1972), in Classical Conditioning II, 64 to 99.

The combination below has no published model that we know of. It is our own construction.

## 3 The model

### 3.1 Experience

- Every Worker carries a learned value v_f for each of the 8 families of
  `designer::FAMILY_STEMS`. Values lie in [−1, 1]. A seed Worker starts at 0 everywhere.
- At birth, a child's Worker copies the values of its source Worker. The sources are those that
  pass on the birth Soul today: the same Worker for a mutation, the first parent's Worker for a
  section crossover, and the prefix and suffix Stages for a stage crossover. A Worker without a
  source starts at 0.
- The values live in the Member, not in the genome, because they change after birth. So the
  genome and the Cascade id stay as today.

### 3.2 Imagination

- Imagination acts on SectionReplace, SectionAdd, SectionDrop and section crossover: the
  operators that can change the families a Worker names.
- The operator is drawn k = 4 times on the same parent. The first draw uses today's random
  stream. Draws 2 to 4 each use their own stream, derived from the seed with the tag
  `imagine/{g}/{i}/{j}`. Here i is the child's index in birth order, as in `soul/{g}/{i}`, and j
  is the draw number, 2 to 4.
- Draw 1 is the child that today's code makes, after all of today's checks (validity,
  admission and novelty). If today's attempt fails, it fails as today, and draws 2 to 4 are not
  made. Each of draws 2 to 4 is one application of the same operator on the same parent, or one
  full section crossover on the same two parents. A draw that fails one of these checks is no
  candidate.
- For each draw, the families that each changed Worker gains and loses are known before any
  model call. A draw can change several Workers: a section crossover crosses every Worker with
  its partner (`src/cascade/variation.rs`, lines 717 to 723).
- The prediction p of a draw is the sum over its changed Workers of (the sum of v_f over the
  Worker's gained families) − (the sum of v_f over its lost families). Each Worker uses the
  values of its source Worker.
- The child is the draw with the highest p. A tie goes to the earliest draw. So while all values
  are 0, the first draw wins, and the Run stays today's Run byte for byte.
- Imagination costs no model call. It only reads the learned values.

### 3.3 Learning from the real effect

- After a newborn is evaluated, its real effect is s = the sign of (its solved count on the
  current goal − its first parent's solved count on the current goal). So s is −1, 0 or +1.
  Both counts use the same tasks; the parent's answers are stored from its birth.
- The error is e = s − p, one number for the newborn. Let n be the number of gained and lost
  families over all its changed Workers. Each gained family moves by +e/(4n), and each lost
  family by −e/(4n). This is the delta rule with rate 1/4, normalized by n, so a change of many
  families takes no larger step than a change of one.
- Values, p and e are kept in integer thousandths. A step is rounded half away from zero:
  step = (e + sign(e) · 2n) / (4n), with integer division towards zero. After each step the value
  is clamped to [−1000, 1000].
- Every newborn learns this way, whichever operator made it. Gained and lost families are those
  of each child Worker against its source Worker. A newborn whose Workers name the same families
  as their sources has n = 0 and learns nothing.
- The child's Workers learn at once. Their source Workers in the parents learn the same steps:
  at the commit of the generation, from each child in birth order, each step clamped on its own.
  This is the owner's "parents and children learn". The change of a parent after its birth is
  logged as an event and replayed by `--verify`, as the `Souls` event is today.

### 3.4 Changing goals

- Every newborn is still evaluated on the whole search panel, as today. So the pass time and
  `rediscovery.json` keep their exact definitions.
- What changes is the goal: the tasks that count for selection and for the real effect s.
  Lexicase uses only the tasks of the current goal as its cases.
- The goals follow from data in the code, not from the solution. G0 is all tasks. G−f is the
  tasks of the targets whose Family strengths (`LibraryTarget.fits`) do not name family f. A G−f
  that equals G0, or is empty, is left out.
- The sequence is G0, G−f1, G0, G−f2, and so on, over the families in index order, and then it
  starts again. Each goal lasts E = 20 generations, as in Kashtan, Noor and Alon (2007).
  Generations 1 to 20 have G0.
- Each variant leaves out the targets that name one family. Most targets name several families,
  so a variant can leave out targets of other modules too: G−Linear keeps only cs and ch. Half of
  the time is spent on the full goal.
- On today's circuit library, this gives seven variants: G−Linear (cs, ch), G−Phase (cz, swap,
  ch), G−Hadamard (swap, cs, ch, ccz), G−Compose and G−Simplify (all targets but fredkin),
  G−Search (all but cz and ch) and G−Meet (all but ch). G−Previous equals G0 and is left out. A
  full cycle is 14 goals, 280 generations.
- Changing goals need lexicase selection and a circuits Domain. Other settings are refused.

### 3.5 What is logged

- The state line gets a group `mind` when imagination or goals are on. It holds the current goal,
  the imagination choices (operator calls with at least one p ≠ 0), the changed picks (the chosen
  draw is not the first), and two mean values over the Workers of this generation's parents: one
  for Linear, Phase and Hadamard, one for the other families.
- The Born event of each newborn holds its values after its own learning step.
- It also holds p, s and the gained and lost families of every newborn whose Workers gained or
  lost families (n > 0), whichever operator made it, and of every newborn that imagination
  chose. A stage operator can add families too, for example when it writes a phrase into a
  copied Worker. The index of the chosen draw is logged only when imagination chose the child.
  So criterion 3 of the screen can be computed from the log alone.

## 6 Risks

- If adding a family rarely changes the solved count, s is mostly 0, and learning is slow.
  Criterion 3 of the screen needs at least 30 informative cases.
- Imagination can exploit wrong values (Jin 2011; Ha and Schmidhuber 2018). Here every imagined
  change is tested for real, and its error corrects the values.
- A goal without a family makes that family useless for 20 generations, so it can be lost.
  Learned values should protect it. Arm G shows the loss without them.
- Values change after birth, so they must survive a resume. They come from the checkpoint and the
  logged events.
